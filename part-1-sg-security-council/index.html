Section 1

The Secretary-General

The Secretary-General is appointed by the General Assembly on the recommendation of the Security Council (Article 97 of the UN Charter). By practice, terms last five years and may be renewed once, the post rotates informally among the regional groups, and no national of a permanent member of the Council has been appointed.

No woman has held the post

All nine Secretaries-General have been men. Four of the seven candidates in the 2026 selection are women.

Eastern Europe has never held it

The Eastern European States group is the only regional group that has never provided a Secretary-General.

Western Europe four times

Norway, Sweden, Austria and Portugal account for four of the nine, more than any other group.

Longest and shortest tenure

U Thant served just over ten years, including his year as acting. Boutros Boutros-Ghali served five, after a United States veto blocked a second term in 1996.

Only one died in office

Dag Hammarskjöld was killed in a plane crash near Ndola, in present-day Zambia, in September 1961, and was awarded the Nobel Peace Prize posthumously.

A Secretary-General, never a Council seat

Myanmar provided U Thant, yet it is one of the 58 Member States never elected to the Security Council.

Section 2

The Security Council

The Council has 15 members: five permanent members with the power of veto, and ten elected by the General Assembly for two-year terms. Five seats are filled each year, candidates need a two-thirds majority, and a retiring member cannot be re-elected immediately (Article 23). Until 1965 the Council had 11 members, six of them elected.

How the 15 seats are shared

Elected seats are allocated by regional group under General Assembly resolution 1991 A (XVIII) of 1963.

By convention, one seat for an Arab state alternates between the African and Asia-Pacific groups.

The permanent five

    China: the seat was held by the Republic of China until 25 October 1971, when General Assembly resolution 2758 (XXVI) recognised the People's Republic of China.

    Russian Federation: continued the seat of the Union of Soviet Socialist Republics from December 1991.

        Japan leads the elected members

        Japan has been elected 12 times (24 years on the Council), followed by Brazil with 11 terms and Argentina with nine.

        Nearly half of Asia-Pacific has never served

        26 of the 58 never-elected Member States are in the Asia-Pacific group, many of them small island states.

        First-timers

        Latvia took its first seat in January 2026. Kyrgyzstan takes its first seat in January 2027.

        The seat that was declined

        Saudi Arabia was elected in October 2013 but declined to take its seat. Jordan was elected in its place for 2014 to 2015.

        Six shared terms

        When no candidate could win, rivals split a two-year term between them six times, most recently Italy (2017) and the Netherlands (2018).

        Home countries of Secretaries-General return

        Austria and Portugal, home countries of Kurt Waldheim and António Guterres, both join the Council in 2027 for a fourth term.

        Method notes

        • A term is each elected period listed by the UN, including one-year terms (1946 and 1966) and split terms, where two countries shared a two-year term.
        • Former Member States (Czechoslovakia, the German Democratic Republic, the United Arab Republic and Yugoslavia) are listed separately, as the UN does. Terms served as Zaire, Ceylon, Upper Volta and the Byelorussian and Ukrainian Soviet Socialist Republics are counted under today's names.
        • The German Democratic Republic's 1980-1981 term is not shown on the UN's elected members page and has been added.
        • Terms for 2027-2028 are included because the members were elected in June 2026.
        • Regional group totals follow current membership (193 Member States).
